PvP matchups

There is no single best PvP build: the answer depends on who you fight. This tab finds the build that does best against a pool of opponents, shows the result per opponent, and tells you who still beats you. Load your replays to use the players you actually meet.

Upload every file in the folder %USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\Turbolento Games\Lootborne\Replays (paste that into the File Explorer address bar, then select all; the game keeps the last 15). Each opponent's gear is read from the replay; their perks and stat points are fitted to the swings. Replays stay in your browser.

Limits of the PvP model

  • Win rates are too high. For Mythic and Ascended builds the model has printed 98 to 100% where real results are lower. Use the ranking and the per-opponent table, not the percentage.
  • Opponents are estimates. Their gear is exact, but perks and stat points are guessed from a single fight, using the perks and stat points in your save now. If you have respecced or changed perks since those fights, the guesses are worse.
  • The pool is small and dated. At most 15 replays, each a snapshot. Players change gear, and matchmaking can pair you with builds that are not in the pool. A build tuned to this pool can lose to a different one; the robust score and the generic archetypes reduce that, they do not remove it.
  • Known stat gap. On at least one gear set the in-game sheet showed about 20% more ATK and 10 more CRIT than this model computes, cause unknown. Your own damage may be understated.
  • Not modelled: Mirror Echo, the PvP boss encounters, and long sudden-death fights (predicted poorly). Guardian Angel, Colossus Hunter and Coup de Grace do nothing in PvP and are left out.
  • Local search. As on the first tab, a better build more than one swap away from every start can be missed.

What the planner simulates

The engine is a swing-by-swing copy of the game's arena combat, built from the decompiled client (builds 3 to 5). A run plays hours of auto-fight: enemy rolls per wave, parry and crit rolls, item effects, perks, deaths that drop you to the previous multiple of five, post-fight regen and stamina rests. A fixed random seed means the same inputs always give the same answer.

  • Stats. Base 400 HP, 8 ATK, 5 DEF, 5% CRIT, 3% PARRY. Per point: +3.2 HP, +0.2 ATK, +0.8 DEF, +0.3% CRIT, +0.25% PARRY. CRIT caps at 60%, PARRY at 55%.
  • A swing. Parry roll, then ATK x 0.8 to 1.2, x2 on a crit, x 60 / (DEF + 60). Lifesteal is capped at 15% and all healing from one swing at 6% of max HP.
  • Arena scaling. With w = wave + 5: enemy HP x (0.0022 w² + 0.1 w + 1.5), ATK x (0.001 w² + 0.06 w + 1.7). Ten kills per wave; bosses at 10, 20, 30 and 40.
  • Drops. 0.75 x (12% blue, 20% purple) per kill plus item bonus (cap 28%), pity at 9 kills. Rarity rows by wave band: 1-10 has no Mythic, 11-20 2%, 21-30 4%, 31+ 6%. Rarity-upgrade gear (cap 24%) promotes one tier.
  • Mythic-equivalents. Mythic drops plus lower drops valued through the forge (25 legendaries make one Mythic, 20 epics one legendary, and so on).
  • Ascended. Counted from reclear rolls only: 0.4% per finished wave on 16-39 and 1% from wave 40, for waves at or below your record. Each Ascended is a uniform pick from 15, so a specific one takes about 15 drops. New-record milestones (every 5 waves: 10% at 40-55, 20% from 60) and the wave-40 boss are one-offs on top.
  • Bloodmarks. Dismantle value of every drop (1 / 3 / 6 / 12 / 20 by rarity) plus automatic PvP (13 per win, 6 per loss, gear bonus capped at +50%). PvP fights are not simulated; set the win rate you actually see. Tradable drops cannot be dismantled in game, so treat the dismantle part as an upper bound.
  • Stamina. 0.8 per battle, then a rest until full. Real hours include those rests; game time runs at 1.25x.

How the search works

It starts from your equipped build and from known archetypes (tank, glass cannon), adapted to your restrictions. For each start it tries every allowed item in one slot, keeps the best, moves to the next slot, then weapons as pairs, perks, stat points and potions, and repeats until nothing improves by more than 1.5%. Candidates are screened on one seed, the top few are confirmed on two, and the finalists are re-run on four fresh seeds for three times as long. The explanations come from the same simulator: undoing one change at a time, removing one perk at a time, and trying single swaps from outside your restrictions.

Where it is weak

  • The model is faster than the game by roughly 1.3x to 1.9x depending on the build, and its highest push ceilings are optimistic. Trust rankings more than absolute numbers.
  • Potion effects for Rampart, Vigor, Fury and Bastion are modelled from their text. Armor Breaker, Sunder Oil and Executioner's Draught are not modelled.
  • Mirror Echo and Rival's Calling are not modelled. A few rare item clauses (temporary HP buffs, DEF shred every N attacks) are ignored.
  • It is a local search: a better build can exist that is more than one swap away from every start. Thorough depth and the "everything" setting reduce that risk.
  • Sector clears are not covered.
  • PvP: both sides use the same swing rules with every item effect and perk, no element bonus, reflect capped at 25%, and sudden death from swing 60. Win rates come out too high and opponents are fitted guesses; the PvP tab lists its limits in full.
