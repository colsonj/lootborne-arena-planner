Limits of the PvP model

  • Win rates are too high. For Mythic and Ascended builds the model has printed 98 to 100% where real results are lower. Use the ranking and the per-opponent table, not the percentage.
  • Opponents are estimates. Their gear is exact, but perks and stat points are guessed from a single fight, using the perks and stat points in your save now. If you have respecced or changed perks since those fights, the guesses are worse.
  • The pool is small and dated. At most 15 replays, each a snapshot. Players change gear, and matchmaking can pair you with builds that are not in the pool. A build tuned to this pool can lose to a different one; the robust score and the generic archetypes reduce that, they do not remove it.
  • Known stat gap. On at least one gear set the in-game sheet showed about 20% more ATK and 10 more CRIT than this model computes, cause unknown. Your own damage may be understated.
  • Not modelled: Mirror Echo, the PvP boss encounters, and long sudden-death fights (predicted poorly). Guardian Angel, Colossus Hunter and Coup de Grace do nothing in PvP and are left out.
  • Local search. As on the first tab, a better build more than one swap away from every start can be missed.

What the planner simulates

The engine is a swing-by-swing copy of the game's arena combat, built from the decompiled client (builds 3 to 5). A run plays hours of auto-fight: enemy rolls per wave, parry and crit rolls, item effects, perks, deaths that drop you to the previous multiple of five, post-fight regen and stamina rests. A fixed random seed means the same inputs always give the same answer.

  • Stats. Base 400 HP, 8 ATK, 5 DEF, 5% CRIT, 3% PARRY. Per point: +3.2 HP, +0.2 ATK, +0.8 DEF, +0.3% CRIT, +0.25% PARRY. CRIT caps at 60%, PARRY at 55%.
  • A swing. Parry roll, then ATK x 0.8 to 1.2, x2 on a crit, x 60 / (DEF + 60). Lifesteal is capped at 15% and all healing from one swing at 6% of max HP.
  • Arena scaling. With w = wave + 5: enemy HP x (0.0022 w² + 0.1 w + 1.5), ATK x (0.001 w² + 0.06 w + 1.7). Ten kills per wave; bosses at 10, 20, 30 and 40.
  • Drops. 0.75 x (12% blue, 20% purple) per kill plus item bonus (cap 28%), pity at 9 kills. Rarity rows by wave band: 1-10 has no Mythic, 11-20 2%, 21-30 4%, 31+ 6%. Rarity-upgrade gear (cap 24%) promotes one tier.
  • Mythic-equivalents. Mythic drops plus lower drops valued through the forge (25 legendaries make one Mythic, 20 epics one legendary, and so on).
  • Ascended. Counted from reclear rolls only: 0.4% per finished wave on 16-39 and 1% from wave 40, for waves at or below your record. Each Ascended is a uniform pick from 15, so a specific one takes about 15 drops. New-record milestones (every 5 waves: 10% at 40-55, 20% from 60) and the wave-40 boss are one-offs on top.
  • Bloodmarks. Dismantle value of every drop (1 / 3 / 6 / 12 / 20 by rarity) plus automatic PvP (13 per win, 6 per loss, gear bonus capped at +50%). PvP fights are not simulated; set the win rate you actually see. Tradable drops cannot be dismantled in game, so treat the dismantle part as an upper bound.
  • Stamina. 0.8 per battle, then a rest until full. Real hours include those rests; game time runs at 1.25x.

How the search works

It starts from your equipped build and from known archetypes (tank, glass cannon), adapted to your restrictions. For each start it tries every allowed item in one slot, keeps the best, moves to the next slot, then weapons as pairs, perks, stat points and potions, and repeats until nothing improves by more than 1.5%. Candidates are screened on one seed, the top few are confirmed on two, and the finalists are re-run on four fresh seeds for three times as long. The explanations come from the same simulator: undoing one change at a time, removing one perk at a time, and trying single swaps from outside your restrictions.

Where it is weak

  • The model is faster than the game by roughly 1.3x to 1.9x depending on the build, and its highest push ceilings are optimistic. Trust rankings more than absolute numbers.
  • Potion effects for Rampart, Vigor, Fury and Bastion are modelled from their text. Armor Breaker, Sunder Oil and Executioner's Draught are not modelled.
  • Mirror Echo and Rival's Calling are not modelled. A few rare item clauses (temporary HP buffs, DEF shred every N attacks) are ignored.
  • It is a local search: a better build can exist that is more than one swap away from every start. Thorough depth and the "everything" setting reduce that risk.
  • Sector clears are not covered.
  • PvP: both sides use the same swing rules with every item effect and perk, no element bonus, reflect capped at 25%, and sudden death from swing 60. Win rates come out too high and opponents are fitted guesses; the PvP tab lists its limits in full.

Do drop rate or item quality stats affect Ascended drops?

No. The milestone roll that hands out Ascended items reads nothing about your character: not "bonus item chance" gear, not "higher rarity drop chance" gear, not Seeker's Luck or any other potion, not your level, perks or stat points. The odds depend on the wave number only. This page shows the decompiled code that proves it and gives the steps to check it yourself.

What actually decides it

  • New-record milestones. Finish a wave that is a multiple of 5 for the first time ever (wave above your record) and you get one item from the table below. The roll is Epic, Legendary, Mythic or Ascended; the Ascended share is the remainder.
  • Reclears. Finish a multiple-of-5 wave at or below your record and there is a small Ascended chance: one fifth of the milestone share, capped at 1%. That is 0.4% on waves 16 to 39 and 1% from wave 40. Nothing else can drop on a reclear.
  • The wave-40 boss. Beating it the first time gives an Ascended item once; bosses 10, 20 and 30 give a Mythic once.
  • Normal kills never give Ascended. The per-kill loot table tops out at Mythic, and the "higher rarity" promotion is capped at that table's top, so it cannot push a Mythic to Ascended.
  • Which Ascended you get is a uniform pick among the Ascended templates, with one re-roll rule: it avoids repeating the last item that dropped in the same slot.
  • No pity, no duplicate protection, no once-per-item rule. The only gate is "wave above record".

MilestonePcts, the whole body

Writes to ArenaLegacyDropTables

A scan of every ISIL method for stores to ItemGenerator static slot +16 and of every decompile for +0xb8)+0x10) = finds the static constructor (sets false) and ArenaSimQA.BandBoundaries (save, set true, assert band maths, restore). One more ISIL hit inside a PlayFab iterator's Reset() is a Cpp2IL artifact; the Ghidra body at that address only throws NotSupportedException. ArenaSimQA.Run has no caller in the binary.

Reproduce it yourself

You need the game folder (Steam, Lootborne, Manage, Browse local files). Only read from it. Two files matter:

Lootborne/GameAssembly.dll
Lootborne/Lootborne_Data/il2cpp_data/Metadata/global-metadata.dat

Step 1 alone, with no Ghidra, already shows the single static read and the absence of stat calls. Step 2 gives readable C for whole function bodies. Step 3 checks the data tables.

Step 1. Cpp2IL stubs and ISIL (about 10 minutes)

Download a Cpp2IL build from github.com/SamboyCoding/Cpp2IL (this used 2022.1.0-pre-release.21) and run it twice:

# class stubs: every field offset and method RVA
./Cpp2IL --game-path "/path/to/Lootborne" --output-as diffable-cs --output-to cpp2il-out
# pseudo-assembly per method, one .txt per class
./Cpp2IL --game-path "/path/to/Lootborne" --output-as isil --output-to isil-out

# the static flag and its slot (expect: public static bool ArenaLegacyDropTables; //Field offset: 0x10)
grep -n "ArenaLegacyDropTables" cpp2il-out/DiffableCs/Assembly-CSharp/AutoBattle/Inventory/ItemGenerator.cs

# the milestone methods and their RVAs
grep -n -B2 -E "MilestonePcts|MilestoneAscendedPct|RollMilestoneRarity|GenerateArenaMilestoneDrop" \
  cpp2il-out/DiffableCs/Assembly-CSharp/AutoBattle/Combat/CombatManager.cs

# ISIL of MilestonePcts: the only memory read is ItemGenerator static +16, then wave compares

  isil-out/IsilDump/Assembly-CSharp/AutoBattle/Combat/CombatManager.txt | grep -n -E "typeof|\[rax\+184\]|\[rax\+16\]|Call"

tools/funcmap.py 
import re,os,glob,sys
base=0x180000000
root=sys.argv[1]; out=open(sys.argv[2],'w'); n=0
for path in glob.glob(root+'/**/*.cs',recursive=True):
    ns=''; stack=[]; pending=None
    for ln in open(path,errors='replace'):
        s=ln.strip(); depth=len(ln)-len(ln.lstrip('\t'))
        m=re.search(r'RVA = "0x([0-9A-Fa-f]+)"',s)
        if m: pending=int(m.group(1),16); continue
        m=re.match(r'namespace ([\w.]+);',s)
        if m: ns=m.group(1); continue
        if s.startswith('[') or s.startswith('//') or not s: continue
        m=re.search(r'\b(?:class|struct|enum|interface) ([\w`<>]+)',s)
        if m and '(' not in s:
            stack=[c for c in stack if c[0]<depth]+[(depth,m.group(1))]; continue
        if pending is not None and '(' in s:
            mm=re.search(r'([\w<>|.`,]+)\s*\(',s)
            if mm:
                cls='+'.join(c[1] for c in stack if c[0]<depth)
                full=(ns+'.' if ns else '')+cls+'.'+mm.group(1)
                full=re.sub(r'[^\w.+<>|`$]','_',full)
                out.write(f"{base+pending:x} {full}\n"); n+=1
            pending=None
out.close(); print(n,'functions')

import ghidra.app.script.GhidraScript;
import ghidra.program.model.address.Address;
import ghidra.program.model.listing.Function;
import ghidra.program.model.symbol.SourceType;
import ghidra.app.cmd.disassemble.DisassembleCommand;
import ghidra.app.cmd.function.CreateFunctionCmd;
import java.io.*;
public class SetupFuncs extends GhidraScript {
  public void run() throws Exception {
    String[] a = getScriptArgs();
    BufferedReader br = new BufferedReader(new FileReader(a[0]));
    String line; int n=0;
    while ((line = br.readLine()) != null) {
      String[] p = line.trim().split(" ");
      if (p.length < 2) continue;
      Address addr = toAddr(Long.parseLong(p[0], 16));
      Function f = getFunctionAt(addr);
      if (f == null) {
        new DisassembleCommand(addr, null, true).applyTo(currentProgram, monitor);
        new CreateFunctionCmd(addr).applyTo(currentProgram, monitor);
        f = getFunctionAt(addr);
      }
      if (f != null) { try { f.setName(p[1], SourceType.USER_DEFINED); } catch (Exception e) {} n++; }
    }
    br.close();
    println("functions set up: " + n);
  }
}

import ghidra.app.script.GhidraScript;
import ghidra.app.decompiler.*;
import ghidra.program.model.listing.Function;
import ghidra.program.model.symbol.*;
import java.io.*;
import java.util.*;
public class DecompList extends GhidraScript {
  public void run() throws Exception {
    String[] a = getScriptArgs();
    File outDir = new File(a[1]); outDir.mkdirs();
    DecompInterface di = new DecompInterface();
    di.setOptions(new DecompileOptions());
    di.openProgram(currentProgram);
    BufferedReader br = new BufferedReader(new FileReader(a[0]));
    String line; int n=0;
    SymbolTable st = currentProgram.getSymbolTable();
    while ((line = br.readLine()) != null) {
      String want = line.trim(); if (want.isEmpty()) continue;
      List<Function> fs = new ArrayList<>();
      SymbolIterator it = st.getSymbolIterator(want + "*", true);
      while (it.hasNext()) { Symbol s = it.next(); Function f = getFunctionAt(s.getAddress()); if (f != null) fs.add(f); }
      if (fs.isEmpty()) { println("NOT FOUND: " + want); continue; }
      for (Function f : fs) {
        DecompileResults r = di.decompileFunction(f, 180, monitor);
        String c = (r != null && r.getDecompiledFunction() != null) ? r.getDecompiledFunction().getC() : "// decompile failed";
        PrintWriter pw = new PrintWriter(new File(outDir, f.getName() + ".c"));
        pw.println("// " + f.getName() + " @ " + f.getEntryPoint());
        pw.println(c); pw.close(); n++;
      }
    }
    br.close(); di.dispose();
    println("decompiled: " + n);
  }
}

How to read IL2CPP decompiles:

  • FUN_1829c5970(0x...) and cRam... = '\x01' blocks at the top are runtime metadata init. Ignore them.
  • *(int *)(X + 0xe4) == 0 followed by func_0x0001829c5cb0() is the static-constructor check for class X. Ignore it.
  • *(T *)(*(longlong *)(CLASS + 0xb8) + OFF) is static field OFF of CLASS. Match OFF to the //Field offset: comment on a static field in the stub.
  • *(T *)(obj + OFF) is an instance field. PlayerState +0x119 endlessMode, +0x124 maxWaveRecord, +0x120 waveKillCount, +0x1c level, +0x90 inventory; ItemInstance +0x2c rarity, +0x50 isNew, +0x58 rarityPromoted; ConsumableModifiers +0x48 dropRateBonus, +0x54 higherRarityBonus.
  • FUN_1821a79a0(0) is UnityEngine.Random.value; FUN_1821a7810(a, b, 0) is Random.Range(int, int).
  • Hex floats: python3 -c "import struct;print(struct.unpack('<f',bytes.fromhex('42640000')[::-1]))" prints 57.0.

What to confirm in decomp_out/:

  1. In the three milestone files the only read not derived from a parameter is (lRam... + 0xb8) + 0x10, on the class whose ToCumBp is called in RollMilestoneRarity. The lRam address changes per build.
  2. GenerateArenaMilestoneDrop.c calls only get_ActiveItemGenerator, get_ActivePlayerState, GenerateUid, NextDropSeed, NewUnseededRng or DropSeedInput_NewRng, RollMilestoneRarity, GenerateItem(..., 1, 0) and Timbra.
  3. ApplyDropProgressionGate.c opens with the +0x119 != 0 early return and ApplyOverlevelDropDampening.c wraps its demotion in +0x119 == 0.
  4. FromTemplate.c is a field-by-field copy with no Random call.
  5. In ApplyBattleOutcome.c the GenerateArenaMilestoneDrop call is guarded by +0x120 > 9, wave % 5 == 0 and +0x124 < wave, inside the else of if ((ps + 0x119 == 0) || (enemy + 0xa0 != 0)).
  6. grep -l "0xb8) + 0x10) = " decomp_out/*.c lists only ArenaSimQA.BandBoundaries.c, and that function restores the value.

Step 3. Metadata tables (2 minutes)

Array initialisers live in global-metadata.dat, not in the DLL. The regular arena rows are 5 floats with no Ascended column, and the boss reward array is 4,4,4,5:

python3 - /path/to/Lootborne/Lootborne_Data/il2cpp_data/Metadata/global-metadata.dat <<'EOF'
import struct,sys
d=open(sys.argv[1],'rb').read()
f=lambda *v: b''.join(struct.pack('<f',x) for x in v)
for row in [(14,22,48,14,2),(10,19,45,20,6)]:          # ARENA_RARITY_TABLE rows 2 and 4
    i=d.find(f(*row)); print(row, hex(i), struct.unpack('<8f', d[i:i+32]))   # 5 values, then unrelated data
p=b''.join(struct.pack('<i',x) for x in (4,4,4,5)); print('boss rarities at', hex(d.find(p)), 'count', d.count(p))
EOF

Step 4. No-tools cross-check

The save file is plain JSON at %USERPROFILE%\AppData\LocalLow\Turbolento Games\Lootborne\save_<steamid>_game.json. A milestone item carries grantSource: 4 and a dropSeed string; a reclear item carries grantSource: 11. maxWaveRecord only moves on a first clear, which is the only milestone gate.

Caveats

  • This is static analysis of the client. The client generates the item and its rarity locally; nothing on this path contacts the server before the rarity is chosen.
  • The boss reward rarities rest on a unique byte-pattern match in metadata plus the code's fallback of Mythic, not a parsed array header.
  • A new Steam build changes RVAs and the lRam class addresses. Method names and field offsets usually survive, so rerun Step 1 and recheck offsets before reusing the numbers above.
